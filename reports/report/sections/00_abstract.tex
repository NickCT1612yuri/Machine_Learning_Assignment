\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

We compare three text-classification pipelines on three public corpora with different label structure: AG News (4 news topics), Rotten Tomatoes (binary sentiment) and Banking77 (77 customer intents). The traditional pipeline uses TF-IDF features with Naive Bayes, logistic regression or a linear SVM; the modern pipeline uses frozen BGE-small sentence embeddings with logistic regression; an optional deep-learning pipeline trains a TextCNN end to end. Features are stored as HDF5 (TF-IDF) and NumPy (BGE) files and read back by the classifiers. For each corpus, 112 TF-IDF configurations (tokenizer, stop words, $n$-grams, classifier, regularisation), 7 BGE configurations and an early-stopped TextCNN are selected on a validation split; each selected model is evaluated once on the official test split, with bootstrap confidence intervals, calibration (Brier score, ECE) and a negation analysis.

Frozen BGE-small achieves the highest test macro-F1 on all corpora (89.2\%, 83.1\%, 92.9\%) and the best calibration. Its advantage over TF-IDF is small and inconclusive on topical news (+1.0\,pp) but clear on sentiment (+5.6\,pp) and intent (+3.5\,pp), where meaning depends on composition and paraphrase. Stop-word removal improves TF-IDF on news but costs 2.9--4.3\,pp on sentiment and intent, because the standard list removes negations and question words. TextCNN, trained without pretraining, trails both feature-based pipelines and is competitive only on Banking77.
